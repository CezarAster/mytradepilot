The rulebook

Five kinds of rule. All of them yours.

You write them while calm, in your own words, with your own numbers. The analyzer drafts; you ratify. Before the session you declare the ones that bind today. The Referee's job is to hold them, not to have opinions about your trades.

Max trades a day

"I stop after 3 trades a day."

Written into TopstepX's own daily trade limit at declaration. Topstep's platform refuses the 4th.

Stop attached

"A stop is on within 15 seconds of every fill. I never widen it."

A fill with no stop at 15 seconds, or a stop moved away from price, is a breach: flatten.

Declared size

"2 contracts. Not 3."

A fill above the declared size is a breach: flatten.

Cooldown

"After 2 losses I stop for 20 minutes."

The next-trade state goes red when the 2nd loss closes; a fill inside the window is a breach: flatten.

No re-entry

"I don't enter within 10 minutes of a losing exit."

Red at the losing exit; a fill inside 10 minutes is a breach: flatten.

The Referee never sets a level or a size. It holds the number you wrote.

Honest statistics

Three lucky trades are not a rule.

Every group's average is shrunk toward your overall average in proportion to how few trades it has. The fewer the trades, the less the engine believes them, and a draft rule cites a number only when the group is solid and its 95% interval excludes your average. Here is the exact rule, on the sample.

adjusted = (n × group avg + 10 × overall avg) ÷ (n + 10)

10 is the number of phantom average trades mixed into every group.

  • Every group's average is shrunk toward your overall average: adjusted = (n × group average + 10 × overall average) ÷ (n + 10). Ten phantom average trades are mixed into every group.
  • Under 5 trades: too few. Faded in every table, never called an edge or a leak. 5 to 14: early signal. 15 or more: solid.
  • An edge needs its shrunk average above your overall average, a positive plain average, and a plain average at least half again above your overall (your average plus half of it).
  • A leak is a group that loses money: its plain average is negative. A weak spot is profitable but averages under half of your overall average.
  • A draft cites a group only when it is solid (15 or more trades) and the 95% interval on its average excludes your overall average. Under that, the draft is still offered, as your rule with no number.

FAQ

Topstep's lockout is free. What is the $19 for?

It runs on my machine. Can't I just close it?

No. It holds the sentence you wrote and the number you put in it. Is the sample real?

No. The sample is a fictional share trader generated by a script in the repository (scripts/make-sample.py) with planted patterns. The Referee runs on TopstepX futures; the Apr 9 scenario applies the Referee's rules to the sample's record to show the mechanics, and says so where it appears. Every number from the sample is computed from that file by the engine, live, in this tab.

Do you see my trades?

No. The file is read in your browser's memory and never sent anywhere. Open the Network tab in your browser's developer tools, drop a file, and watch nothing go out. The only things this site ever sends are your email address, your platform and whether you have a partner in mind, if you join the waitlist.

Is the analyzer really free?

Yes. It runs on your computer, so it costs nothing to run and stays free, with rule drafting, one manual rule, a private rules-kept score and the Record Card. The Referee is $19 a month or $169 a year, and is not for sale until the pilot has run.

Can I check the math?

Yes. The engine lives in src/core with tests in test/, and the command-line tool prints the same report and the same three drafts: npm run report -- --tz America/New_York your-trades.csv.
