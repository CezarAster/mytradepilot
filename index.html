How it works

From fills to verdict in four scrolls.

It reads your fills.

Any broker CSV with symbol, side, quantity, price, time. TradingView trade lists too. Fills are matched into round trips, duplicates dropped.

It tags every trade.

New York entry time, trade number of the day, what came before, hold time, direction, weekday, ticker.

It finds where the money is.

Your 1st trade of the day: 45 trades, 73% win, +0.94% a trade vs +0.33% overall. Solid.

And where it leaks.

Your 3rd trade of the day: 26 trades, 42% win, −0.81% a trade. Skipping them would have made your total +58.5% instead of +37.3%.

Hold time is shown but never mined: winners get held longer because they are winning.

Honest statistics

Three lucky trades are not an edge.

Every group's average is shrunk toward your overall average in proportion to how few trades it has. The fewer the trades, the less the engine believes them. Here is the exact rule, on the sample.

adjusted = (n × group avg + 10 × overall avg) ÷ (n + 10)

10 is the number of phantom average trades mixed into every group.

  • Every group's average is shrunk toward your overall average: adjusted = (n × group average + 10 × overall average) ÷ (n + 10). Ten phantom average trades are mixed into every group.
  • Under 5 trades: too few. Faded in every table, never called an edge or a leak. 5 to 14: early signal. 15 or more: solid.
  • An edge needs its shrunk average above your overall average, a positive plain average, and a plain average at least half again above your overall (your average plus half of it).
  • A leak is a group that loses money: its plain average is negative. A weak spot is profitable but averages under half of your overall average.

Read the rule in src/core/analyze.ts →

Your stop

Your stop, replayed.

Every trade's worst point is checked against your stop. Three things can happen. On the sample, 33 trades went more than 2% against this trader and 7 of them came back green. Closing every one at −2% would have made +13.2% instead of +37.3%. The stop isn't the leak. The third trade is.

A tighter stop is not automatically better. The check reports what yours did, not what a rule says.

FAQ

Questions, answered straight.

Is it really free?

Yes. The analyzer runs on your computer, not on our servers, so it costs nothing to run and it stays free. The waitlist is for the full app, which will be paid.

Do you see my trades?

No. The file is read in your browser's memory and never sent anywhere. You can check: open the Network tab in your browser's developer tools, drop a file, and watch nothing go out. The only thing this site ever sends is your email address, if you join the waitlist.

Is the sample real?

No. The sample is a fictional trader generated by a script in the repository (scripts/make-sample.py) with planted patterns, so you can see what the analyzer finds. Every number on this page is computed from that file by the engine, live, in this tab.

Which files work?

TradingView's List of trades export from Bar Replay or the Strategy Tester, and any broker executions or fills export with symbol, side, quantity, price and time columns. Several files at once are fine: overlapping exports are de-duplicated and partial fills are merged.

Which markets?

Anything your export covers: stocks, futures, forex, crypto. The entry-time buckets use New York market hours; trade number of the day, what came before, direction, hold time, weekday and ticker don't depend on the session.

How many trades do I need?

It runs on a handful, but under 30 trades the whole report is labelled an early signal. Groups under 5 trades are faded and never called an edge or a leak; 5 to 14 is an early signal; 15 or more is solid.

What does "early signal" mean?

Fewer than 15 trades in the group. "Solid" is 15 or more. Under 5 is "too few" and never becomes an insight.

What does "shrunk toward your average" mean?

Each group's average is mixed with 10 imaginary trades at your overall average: adjusted = (n × group average + 10 × overall average) ÷ (n + 10). Three lucky trades at +4% read as about +1.2%; 45 trades at +0.9% keep most of their signal.

Why isn't hold time used for edges?

Because winners get held longer precisely because they are winning. It's shown so you can see it, but mining it would just tell you to hold winners, which you already know. Ticker and weekday are kept out for a different reason: they are too easy to overfit.

What is the stop check?

If your export includes each trade's worst point (TradingView's does; a fills export can add a Worst % column), the report counts trades that went past your usual stop and came back, the ones that didn't, and what your total would be if every one had been closed at the stop. Without worst-point data the check is skipped and the report says so.

Can I check the math?

Yes. The engine lives in src/core with tests in test/, and the command-line tool prints the same report as the page: npm run report -- --tz America/New_York your-trades.csv.

What's in the full app, and when? Is any of this advice?

Journal, edge analysis that stays current, a pre-trade check, live alerts for your best setups and an AI review of your week. No launch date is promised; waitlist members get early access and founding-member pricing. None of it is investment advice: it is an educational tool, and past results don't predict future results.
